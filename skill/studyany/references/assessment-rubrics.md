# Assessment And Reporting Rubrics

Use these rubrics to make progress claims proportional to evidence. Scores are
descriptors, not a judgment of the learner. Use the separate learning
regulation reference when time, pace, drift, or explicit strain requires a
coaching adjustment.

## Evidence dimensions

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding | cannot state the idea | recognizes terms | explains with gaps | accurate explanation | explains limits and connections |
| Retrieval | no recall | recalls after cue | partial unaided recall | accurate unaided recall | accurate recall after delay |
| Application | cannot start | follows an example | completes a similar task with help | independent similar task | independent changed task |
| Transfer | no connection | recognizes a familiar pattern | adapts with help | adapts independently | selects and justifies a strategy in a new context |
| Retention | no delayed evidence | large loss after delay | partial retention | mostly stable retention | stable across varied delayed checks |

Use `null` when a dimension was not assessed. Do not turn a missing dimension
into zero.

## Mastery levels

Use the lowest important dimension as a conservative ceiling:

```text
0 - not encountered or no evidence
1 - exposure or recognition only
2 - understands with cues or completes a worked example
3 - handles a similar task independently
4 - handles changed cases and explains decisions
5 - stable transfer, teaching, or high-quality production evidence
```

Level 5 is not required for every goal. The target level should come from the
goal and success evidence. A learner can complete a survey with level 2 to 3
evidence, while a professional task may require level 4 or 5.

## Stage gates

Before marking a stage complete, check:

- core concepts have retrieval evidence;
- at least one independent task is complete;
- at least one changed example or transfer check is attempted when relevant;
- critical prerequisites have no unresolved repeated misconception;
- evidence is not based only on the same example used during teaching.

When a gate fails, report the exact missing evidence and choose the smallest
remediation task.

## Confidence calibration

When confidence was recorded before an answer, compare it with the result:

- high confidence plus incorrect result: add prediction and error-analysis
  practice;
- low confidence plus correct result: show the evidence and gradually raise
  challenge;
- high confidence plus correct result: test delayed retrieval or transfer;
- low confidence plus incorrect result: reduce task size and repair the
  prerequisite.

Do not use confidence as a score of ability.

## Learning curve and feedback

Describe a learning curve as a set of evidence trends, not one global feeling
or one total score. Compare at least two reasonably similar observations when
possible:

- retrieval across a delay;
- application on a similar task;
- transfer to a changed context;
- retention after an interval;
- time and workload needed for comparable performance.

Useful temporary trajectory labels are `building`, `consolidating`,
`fragile`, `stalled`, `recovering`, and `insufficient_data`. They describe the
current evidence pattern, not a learner identity. Do not call a curve positive
merely because minutes, confidence, or immediate correctness increased. Do not
call it negative merely because one difficult review failed.

When giving feedback, state the observed evidence, the learning meaning, the
one adjustment, and the next check. Credit a strategy, attempt, correction, or
repeated practice. Avoid generic praise, ability labels, and psychological
diagnoses.

## Report format

### Daily summary

```text
Study time: <actual minutes, with source>
Evidence: <recall/application/transfer result>
Improved: <specific concept or behavior>
Unresolved: <specific error or uncertainty>
Next action: <one task>
Review due: <date or unknown>
```

### Weekly review

Include:

- planned versus actual time;
- number of sessions and effective frequency;
- retrieval and delayed-review trend;
- application or production evidence;
- recurring errors and likely causes;
- overdue review priority;
- one roadmap adjustment;
- a realistic next-week commitment.

Avoid declaring a positive learning curve when only time or confidence
increased. Say when evidence is sparse or incomparable.

When the deterministic analytics projection is available, include its data
quality, dimension labels, sample counts, and deltas in the report. Treat
`building`, `consolidating`, `fragile`, `stalled`, and `recovering` as
temporary evidence patterns. A configured time target may explain workload,
but it cannot raise an evidence dimension without an assessment, delayed
review, independent task, or transfer result.

### Stage report

State the goal, current stage, evidence for each gate, unresolved risks, and
the recommended next stage. Use language such as `evidence suggests level 3`
rather than `you have mastered this` when evidence is limited.
